# Immesive journalism

Paljudest teemadest (alkohol, mets, poliitika, autod, maksud) on üha keerulisem rääkida. Mitte sellepärast, et keegi keelaks, vaid sellepärast, et inforuum on üle uputatud kobar-eelnõudest, PR-jutupunktidest, hõimu- ja identiteedipõhistest raevust ning lihtsalt inimestest, kes mängivad populaarsuse huvides multikategelasi.
Ühesõnaga, avalda selles müras olulisi mõtteid kui tahad, kuid kedagi ei koti! Neid loevad ainult sotsid. Kuidas siis luua lugusid, mis võtaks pisut müra maha ning aitaks ühendada inimesi lahenduse otsimisel?

Inglise keeles on sügavalt osaleva ajakirjanduse nimi [immersive journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immersive_journalism#:~:text=Immersive%20Journalism%20is%20a%20form,news%20reports%20and%20documentary%20film.). Ka Levila harrastab olukordadesse sukeldumist ning läbi sealt saadud taipamiste uut moodi ajakirjandusliku kogemuse loomist.

Oluline on mitte karta seda, mida sa teada saad. Oluline on leida teistsuguseid väljendusvahendeid. Näiteks, sel nädalal läheb Levila ka tricksteriks ning teeb siiralt ettepanekuid, mis aitavad probleeme märgata või lahendada. ## Endale või teistele?

There has been research done on the relationship between happiness and self-care versus caring for others. Here's a summary of what some scientific studies have found:
Self-Care and Happiness:
* Taking time for self-care activities like exercise, getting enough sleep, and engaging in hobbies has been linked to increased well-being and life satisfaction (Hampikian, 2006; Reizer, 2019).
* Practicing self-compassion and self-acceptance is associated with lower levels of anxiety, depression, and negative emotions (Neff, 2011).
* However, excessive self-focus and narcissistic tendencies can undermine happiness (Twenge & Campbell, 2009).

Caring for Others and Happiness:
* Performing acts of kindness and helping others has been shown to increase positive emotions and life satisfaction (Lyubomirsky et al., 2005; Dunn et al., 2008).
* People who engage in volunteering experience greater happiness, life satisfaction, and better physical health compared to non-volunteers (Brown et al., 2003; Borgonovi, 2008).
* Having strong social connections and feeling a sense of relatedness with others are fundamental human needs linked to well-being (Deci & Ryan, 2000).

Overall, the research suggests that both self-care and caring for others can contribute to happiness, but in different ways:
Self-care provides personal fulfillment, stress relief, and a sense of self-worth, which are important for well-being.
Caring for others creates positive social bonds, a sense of meaning/purpose, and activates the brain's reward system associated with charitable behavior (Moll et al., 2006).
Many experts argue that true happiness comes from striking a balance between self-care and caring for others - nurturing yourself while also contributing to something larger than yourself (Seligman, 2011; Brown & Wong, 2017).
So in essence, incorporating both self-care and caring for others into one's life may lead to the greatest levels of overall life satisfaction and sustained happiness.
